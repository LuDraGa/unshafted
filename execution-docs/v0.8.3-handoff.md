### Then: the browse view

The design is settled in
[`site-corpus-browse-design.md`](site-corpus-browse-design.md). **Read it before writing anything** —
it exists because the obvious layout does not survive contact with the corpus, and the finding is
cheap to re-derive wrongly.

The single most important thing in it: **absent disclosures cannot be a section.** 35 of 37 domains
have at least one, so a section for them is not a finding, it is the default — the same failure the
handoff already worries about for the badge being red on 36 of 37. The clock (19 of 37) is the one
boolean that splits the corpus.

## 3. Rules this work ran into, and that still hold

- **Silence is not absence.** `requiredDisclosures` records what each analysis found worth
  recording, not a systematic checklist. A count of absent disclosures is a lower bound on findings,
  never a measurement of the document — which is why the browse row shows no tally and why
  "browse by what is missing" is rejected until Pass 2 canonicalises the names.
- **Never claim a window is live.** `relative_to_signup` needs the date the reader accepted, which
  we do not have, and D14 exists because inferring an anchor got done once already.
- **One property, one spelling.** `hasTimeSensitiveAction` keeps the name it has in
  `policy-seed.json` and `corpus-bundle.ts`. #82 is the whole lesson.
- **A field with no consumer does not ship.** #79 deleted six tokens for it; the manifest carries
  three fields for the same reason.
- **The badge path makes no network call, ever.** Browse adds none.
