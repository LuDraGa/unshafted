## 1 · The tablist contract was advertised, not honoured

`LensStrip` declared `role="tablist"`, each button `role="tab"` with `aria-selected` and
`aria-controls`, and the panel `role="tabpanel"`. All correct, and all of it a promise: a screen
reader announces "tab list, Blockers, 1 of 5" and a keyboard user reasonably expects ←/→ to move
between them.

There was no `onKeyDown` anywhere in the popup. Nothing moved. And because every tab was in the tab
order, Tab walked through all five one at a time before reaching the panel — the exact behaviour
the tablist role exists to replace.

An ARIA role that lies is worse than no role at all: the un-roled version would at least have been
honest about being a row of buttons.

Fixed with the standard pattern, both halves of it:

- **Roving tabindex.** The selected tab is `tabIndex=0`, the rest `-1`. Tab now enters the strip
  once and leaves it once.
- **←/→ wrap, Home/End jump.** Selection follows focus, which is the right choice here because
  switching a lens is free — no request, no loss of state — so there is nothing to protect the
  user from triggering by arrowing past it.

`aria-controls` already pointed at the panel, so nothing about the announcement changed; it just
became true.

**The handler sits on the tabs, not on the strip, and `jsx-a11y` is what made that decision.** The
obvious placement is the container — events bubble, one handler instead of five. `eslint` rejected
it: *"Elements with the 'tablist' interactive role must be focusable"*. That reads like a false
positive on a roving tabindex, where the container must **not** be a tab stop, and it is not one:
a key handler on an element that can never hold focus can only ever fire for a keypress that
started somewhere else. The tabs are the focusable things, so the handler belongs on them. The rule
was pointing at a real incoherence, not at a pattern it failed to recognise.

## 2 · The panel could still grow without bound

`CollapsibleItem` is a `<details>`, and every item in a lens could be open at once. Open six
blockers and the panel grows six bodies tall — capped only by `max-height: 60vh` and an internal
scrollbar.

That is the accordion stack the lens architecture was built to replace, reintroduced one level
down. The north star in the v0.10 spec is *"switching question swaps content in place — never
pushes the page"*, and it held for lenses while quietly failing for the items inside one.

The fix is one attribute. `<details name="...">` is the platform's own exclusive accordion —
opening one closes its siblings, no state, no handler, no JS. Every item in a lens shares
`name={lens-${id}}`, scoped per lens so two lenses cannot collide (only one renders at a time
anyway, but the scoping costs nothing and documents the intent).

Chrome 120 shipped this in December 2023. The extension is Chrome-only and sets no
`minimum_chrome_version`, so the floor is Chrome's own support for Manifest V3 features already in
use — well past 120.

Worth naming what this gives up: a user can no longer hold two findings open to compare them. In a
440px popup they could not usefully see both anyway, and a panel that cannot bound its own height
is the more expensive failure.

## 3 · Deep analysis was a dead end for scope

`showCtaBar` was `quick && !deep && !running`. Once deep landed, the bar unmounted — and the cog
that opens `ScopeSheet` lives on the bar. So after a deep run there was no way to change the role
or the priorities. The only route was to upload the file again.

This came from the locked spec, which called deep "the terminal state" and had the bar vanish. That
is right about the *analysis* and wrong about the *controls*: the scope is not a property of the
run, it is a property of how the user wants to read the document, and wanting to re-read it as a
different party is an ordinary thing to want.

So the bar now survives deep. It keeps the scope summary and the cog, and the action reads
**Re-run analysis** instead of **Run analysis**. `handleDeepAnalysis` needed no change — it guards
on `deep-running` and on a missing quick scan, never on a deep result already existing, so a second
run overwrites cleanly.

One consequence to keep straight: `scopeOpen` is derived as `scopeOpenFor === currentAnalysis.id`,
and its comment credited `showCtaBar` with closing the sheet when deep arrives. That is no longer
true and the comment has been corrected rather than left to mislead. The keying by analysis id
still does the job it was actually written for — a new document closes the sheet.

## 4 · Motion, and 25 rules that painted nothing

**Reduced motion.** No `prefers-reduced-motion` query existed on any surface. The popup animates a
chevron rotation, a hover lift on lens tabs, a skeleton pulse and a spinner; Options and the side
panel carry a handful of transitions each.

The block added to `global.css` covers all three surfaces at once — it belongs there rather than in
`Popup.css` because the preference is not a popup concern.

**Every animation stops, and each one gets a resting state chosen for it.** The instinct was to
exempt the spinner — the blanket `animation-duration: 0.01ms !important` recipe in wide circulation
freezes it mid-arc, and a stuck spinner reads as hung rather than calm. But the repo had already
answered this: `SidePanel.css` carries a `prefers-reduced-motion` block for `.panel-busy-dot` that
stops its pulse and rests it at `opacity: 0.7`. The dot keeps saying "working" without moving to say
it, which is the right shape, and the spinner gets the same treatment — a whole ring in the brand
colour instead of a turning arc. Transitions are zeroed by DURATION rather than removed, so state
still changes; an open chevron still points down, just instantly.

**The onboarding arrow is the trap in this block.** `spotlight-bounce-arrow` composes its bounce
with the caller's rotation — `translateY(…) var(--spotlight-arrow-rotate)` — so the rotation exists
*only inside the keyframe*. `animation: none` alone would have left the arrow unrotated, pointing at
nothing, on every surface that runs the tour. It is re-applied explicitly.

**Why the block lives in `global.css`.** The preference is not a popup concern, and one block that
names every animation in the product is harder to forget than three that each name a third of them.
The cost is real and small: `global.css` ships to all three pages, so Options and the side panel each
carry one `.popup-spinner` rule for a class they never render — about sixty bytes apiece. Splitting
the block per page would save that and make it easy to add an animation on one surface and miss it.

**Dead rules.** Four families, verified by grepping every `.tsx` on the surface: zero consumers
each.

| family | rules | died with |
|---|---:|---|
| `.popup-chip*` | 11 | `ChipRow`, replaced by `LensStrip` |
| `.popup-accordion*` | 10 | `ResultAccordion`, replaced by `CollapsibleItem` |
| `.popup-top-blockers*` | 2 | `TopBlockersInline`, now the Blockers lens |
| `.popup-verdict-strip*` | 2 | the pre-compact verdict |

All four were left in place deliberately in the v0.10 pass to keep that diff readable, and recorded
as owed. This is the payment. A comment marks where they were, in the same style as the
`.popup-alert*` note #84 left one screen above — the point of these markers is that the next person
grepping for a class name finds out it was removed on purpose rather than wondering whether they
broke something.

## 5 · #86 — the tint moves to the badge

The row renders a required disclosure a policy does not contain — a retention period, a grievance
contact, a notice-of-changes clause that GLBA, CCPA, GDPR or COPPA says should be there. It was
hardcoded `border-rose-200 bg-rose-50`, which after #82 is **risk Low** exactly: the grade meaning
*this document treats you well*, painting a missing statutory notice.

**The wall question was settled before this work started:** an absent disclosure is a finding about
the document, not the app talking about itself, so it stays on the tinted side of #84 — rose, not
`.unshafted-danger-tone`. That was the decision #86 asked for.

**Which rose is where the issue's own second criterion decides it.** #86 requires the row not be
lighter than the card it sits inside. The graded element in that card is the summary box, which
carries `RISK_TONE[riskLevel]` — `bg-rose-200` at High, `bg-rose-300` at Very High. And
`--unshafted-severity-high-bg` is `rose-200`. So filling the row with the severity token would make
it *identical* to a High summary box and *lighter* than a Very High one — failing the criterion on
exactly the documents where a missing disclosure matters most, and putting a second full-fill rose
vocabulary beside the grade fill, which is the collision #82 spent a release removing.

**So the fill is not where the tint belongs.** `ExposureRow`, the canonical document finding in this
same file, already answers this: a neutral `.panel-row` carrying a **severity badge**, with
`SEVERITY_TONE` tinting the pill and nothing else. The absent-disclosure row now has that shape —
`.panel-row`, a `MISSING` badge at `--unshafted-severity-high-*`.

This satisfies all three of #86's boxes and adds nothing:

- it no longer renders in the tone that means Low;
- it cannot be lighter than the summary box, because it no longer competes on fill weight at all;
- it routes through a token instead of hardcoded rose utilities.

And it closes the question #86 raised about `--unshafted-severity-high-border` — **no such token is
needed.** #78 declined to add one because nothing consumed it, and that reasoning survives intact
rather than expiring, which is the better outcome for a palette that has spent three issues
shedding tokens.

**What this does not fix.** The handoff's complaint that an absent disclosure is *buried* — below
the fold, under "Missing disclosures", after exposures and actions — is a placement problem, and
placement is not what #86 asked about. It stays open for the signal work.

## The popup had no tests. It does now.

`pages/options` and `pages/side-panel` both have vitest suites; `pages/popup` had none — no config,
no `test` script, no directory. Items 1 and 2 are exactly the kind of thing that cannot be checked
by looking: the roles in the JSX read as correct whether or not anything listens for a key, and
`<details name>` is one attribute whose absence changes nothing visible until six items are open.

So the popup got the same three-file setup the other two pages use — `vitest.config.ts`, a
`test/tsconfig.json`, and the `test` script — plus `test/lens-strip.test.tsx`, six assertions over
`ResultsView` rendered from `sampleQuickScan`:

- exactly one tab is in the page tab order, and it is the selected one;
- ←/→ move selection *and* focus, wrapping at both ends;
- Home/End jump to the ends;
- any other key falls through un-prevented;
- each tab's `aria-controls` and the panel's `aria-labelledby` actually point at each other;
- every item in a lens shares one `<details name>`, scoped to that lens.

**The assertions were checked against the bug, not just against the fix.** Stripping the three
mechanisms back out — `onKeyDown`, `tabIndex`, `group` — fails exactly four of the six. The two that
survive are the two describing behaviour that was already correct before this work, which is the
result that makes the other four trustworthy.

One honest limit: **jsdom does not implement the exclusive-accordion behaviour of `<details name>`.**
That belongs to the browser, and Chrome has had it since 120. The test asserts the mechanism is
present and correctly scoped per lens, which is the half that can regress in this repo; the closing
itself is Chrome's to get right and stays on the manual list.
